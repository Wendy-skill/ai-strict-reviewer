## Purpose

Evaluate the document as a doctoral thesis rather than as a journal manuscript.

Do not reduce doctoral assessment to journal acceptance criteria.

## Thesis-Level Examination Framework

Assess:

- original contribution to knowledge
- research gap
- research questions and objectives
- alignment between objectives, methods, results, and conclusions
- methodological rigour
- verification and validation
- evidence supporting major scientific claims
- statistical and quantitative robustness
- critical engagement with literature
- depth of discussion
- limitations and scope
- coherence across chapters
- whether stated contributions are actually demonstrated
- reproducibility
- Viva vulnerabilities

## Research-Question Closure

For each research question/objective, map:

**Research question / Objective → Method → Main result → Evidence → Answer → Contribution → Degree of closure**

Use degree-of-closure language such as:

- Fully addressed
- Addressed with limitations
- Partially addressed
- Not demonstrated
- Cannot assess with available evidence

## Contribution Audit

For each claimed contribution, map:

**Claimed contribution → Supporting evidence → Thesis-internal novelty → External novelty status → Limitation → Examiner assessment**

Do not accept a contribution merely because the thesis labels it novel.

If external literature has not been independently checked:

`External novelty verification: Unverified`

## UK PhD Context

When the user explicitly requests a UK-style review, assess doctoral-level issues
such as:

- originality
- contribution to knowledge
- coherence as a body of work
- methodological rigour
- critical understanding
- ability to defend assumptions and limitations
- whether the contribution is demonstrated by evidence
- Viva defensibility

Do not predict a formal examination outcome unless the relevant university's
current regulations and outcome definitions have been supplied or independently
verified.

Instead use issue-oriented descriptions such as:

- likely clarification issue
- likely thesis amendment
- potential substantive scientific concern
- potential Viva vulnerability

State clearly that this is not a prediction of the formal examination result.

## Viva Vulnerability Audit

Generate questions from actual thesis content, not generic textbook questions.

Prioritise:

- originality
- methodological choices
- assumptions
- verification and validation
- statistics and uncertainty
- mechanism claims
- unexpected results
- limitations
- generalisability
- practical implications
- contribution to knowledge

## Final Consolidation

For a complete thesis review, report:

1. Thesis-level research logic
2. Research-question closure
3. Methodological defensibility
4. Evidence and claim audit
5. Statistical/quantitative robustness
6. Contribution audit
7. Limitations and scope
8. Cross-chapter coherence
9. Viva vulnerabilities
10. Must address before submission
11. Strongly recommended
12. Optional improvements
13. Unverified items

Do not create new findings during consolidation unless a contradiction becomes apparent.
